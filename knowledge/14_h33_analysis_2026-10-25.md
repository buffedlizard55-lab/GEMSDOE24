## What the local forensic evidence supports

See `evidence/peer_forensics_20261025.json`,
`evidence/priority_review_20261025.json`, and `registry/live_scores.json`.
These are recorded local measurements, not a new forensic run in this review.
The recorded GEMSDOE32 artifact has 37,654 positive pixels. Its positive set is
the 44,090-dot GEMSDOE25 set after removing 6,436 dots within 200 m of the supplied
catalogue. Relative to the 40,199-dot GEMSDOE31 set, 2,545 dots are removed.
There are no newly added prediction coordinates in that comparison.

That geometric ancestry does **not** reveal hidden-truth detections, permeability,
vents, or hidden-set TP/FP. A removed dot can carry both useful true-positive
credit and false-positive mass. No evidence here proves that all improvement
was false-positive reduction, that a particular emission budget is optimal,
that thinning has saturated, or that only one new geological strategy can win.

## A testable explanation, not a score forecast

The shared evaluator uses distance-weighted Tversky with alpha 0.2, beta 0.8,
and a 300 m triangular kernel. Sparse dots can reduce prediction mass while
retaining some nearby truth credit. Catalogue clearance may remove redundant
flank predictions. These are plausible explanations for the owner-reported
ordering, not established hidden-set causal effects.

Let T=TPw, F=FPw and N=truth mass, with FNw=N-T. Then the denominator is
0.2T+0.2F+0.8N. Removing positive contributions t and f improves the ratio
only if `t*(0.2*F+0.8*N) < 0.2*T*f` (with positive denominators).
Neither t nor f is available for the competition hidden truth. Hence we cannot
infer the benefit of a removal from catalogue distance alone. In particular,
fewer emitted dots does not by itself establish an improved score.
