## What GEMSDOE32 B2 can and cannot tell us

The [artifact ancestry audit](../evidence/portfolio_artifact_audit_20261026.json)
compares exact masks, not hidden truth. In the saved comparison GEMSDOE25/30's
D2.8 mask has 44,090 dots; B2 has 37,654, with 6,436 removed within 2 px of

pixels gives the same B2. These are two different parents, not a numerical
contradiction. The exact B2 SHA-256 in the pinned audit is
`c55bafc470054e8271dcb89347a17e07fefe50de6af6e6ba6c4b169ef7ab6fa9`;
some old narrative gives a different digest and must not be used for identity.
This review did not re-run the complete historical ancestry calculation.

Owner-reported `0.2778` has no artifact-linked organizer receipt. Its apparent
increase could reflect reduced false-positive mass after pruning, but the hidden
true-positive credit and false-positive loss are unknown. A near-catalogue dot
may correctly predict a new fault or correction; no proximity rule guarantees
it is false. With local pooled true credit `T`, false-positive mass `F`, and
withheld truth mass `G`, the documented DTI is
`T / (0.2*T + 0.2*F + 0.8*G)`. Deleting predictions with lost true credit `t`
and removed FP mass `f` helps only when
`t*(0.2*F + 0.8*G) < 0.2*T*f`, ignoring the small epsilon. In particular, a
dot can affect multiple truth pixels: `t` is not a Bernoulli hit rate. See
[corrected metric analysis](../docs/metric-integrity-20261026.md). Do not use
an assumed hidden label mass to turn this into a causal proof or score forecast.

The organizer's public staff clarification says known-fault pixels are
pixel-exactly excluded and new truth can lie within 300 m of a known trace;
nearby unmasked predictions are not automatically free:
[staff clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4).
A later clarification says Phase 2 labels may be added, removed or changed,
the known-fault exclusion is retained, and the same metric applies:
[staff final-round clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/questions-about-the-final-round-rules/11556/3).
Neither clarification exposes hidden labels or verifies an individual score.
