## 2. Why H33-B2 leads, and what beating it requires (PhD-level read of the evidence)

Forensics (`evidence/priority_review_20261025.json`) are exact: H33-B2 = H27-4's
40,199 dots with the 2,545 dots ≤2 px from the supplied catalogue deleted; **0 dots
added**. The owner-reported ladder on one ancestry — continuous ridge 0.1922 →
dotted 0.2477/0.2600 → solo 0.2708 → catalogue-clearance 0.2778 — therefore isolates
**emission efficiency**, not detection. Under DTI = TPw/(TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw)
(300 m triangular kernel; organizer metric, `src/gems/metric.py`), a 1-px dot retains
kernel credit 1.00/0.67/0.33 at 0/1/2 px from truth while emitting far less FP mass
than a continuous ridge whose localization error makes most of its length FP-weighted.
Dots on the catalogued apron can earn no hidden-truth credit (known faults are masked
by the organizer) yet still carry FP risk, so deleting them is the cheapest DTI
available. The inequality in `knowledge/14_h33_analysis_2026-10-25.md` shows pruning
helps only when lost TP credit < α·D·ΔF/[1−(1−β)D]; hidden TP/FP of the deleted dots
are unobservable, so "pure FP savings" remains an explanation, not a proof.

Consequence: the emission side is near its local optimum (≈37.7k dots, ≥200 m
off-catalogue, ≥250 m spacing — exactly the shared `submodular_dti_allocate`
operating point). **Beating 0.2778 requires a better ranking surface** — specifically
mass reallocated from already-mapped range fronts (where hidden marginal truth
density is low) to catalogue-incomplete basin/piedmont structures (where it is high) —
**without** losing dense-holdout skill. That is a label-shift problem: supervised
models trained on the mapped catalogue inherit its range-front sampling bias, and the
dense segment holdout rewards the same bias. This is why the SGMC sparse
off-catalogue proxy is reported alongside every dense number below.

## 4. Scientific lessons

1. **Incremental supervised stacks converge.** Adding channels to the GVC feature
   family reproduces GVC's dots (91%) regardless of the new physics, because the
   allocator's argmax set is stable. Any future lane that includes GVC18+HGB will
   fail novelty vs GVC1/CUP1 by construction. A promotable lane must be an
   *orthogonal surface family* (e.g. preregistered ranks 3/5: tip-damage coherence
   drop, drainage-deflection graph) or a differently-regularized learner — and must
   still beat 0.15038 on the same evaluator.
2. **Compositional fronts carry real but small signal.** CUP channels improved
   3/4 folds (largest in SW, +0.0082) with clean canaries — consistent with
   alteration-related K/Th/U redistribution — yet the effect is within paired
   noise and vanishes on SGMC. Mimic risk (lithologic contacts, arkosic fronts)
   remains the leading alternative explanation.
3. **Dense-vs-sparse disagreement persists.** Dense holdout ranks CUP1 first;
   SGMC ranks it last of the three. Until a candidate wins on *both*, live-score
   claims would be speculation. No geothermal-vent claim follows from any fault
   raster here.

## 5. Why the Rank-2 fallback was not run

Relief-stratified allocation acts on the same CUP surface; to pass novelty it would
need to move >30% of dots >3 px from both GVC1 and CUP1 while staying
dense-noninferior — mutually unlikely given §4.1, and it would have consumed the
remaining verification budget. It remains preregistered for a future session
**only** if paired with an orthogonal surface.
