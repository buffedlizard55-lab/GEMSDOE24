4 · Why H33-B2 (GEMSDOE32, 0.2778) scored high — evidence-bounded

  1. Arithmetic (from the official definition, as implemented in src/gems/metric.py). With FN_w = N − TP_w, DTI = TP_w / (0.8·N + 0.2·(TP_w + FP_w)). Removing a dot raises the score only if its credit-to-FP-weight ratio is below roughly 0.2·TP/(0.8N + 0.2·FP).
  2. Verified set fact. B2 has 37,654 dots and is an exact subset of the 44,090-dot GEMSDOE25 D2.8 file. The 6,436 removed dots are all 1–3 px from the catalogue, and none sits on it. B2 keeps 5.8% of its dots within 3 px of the catalogue; D2.8 has 19.5% (evidence/b2_subset_forensics_20261030.json).
  3. Mechanism (plausible, not proven). The competition's labels are new faults, so a dot hugging an already-mapped trace plausibly earns little credit yet carries a full penalty. Pruning such dots lowers FP mass with little TP loss. Owner-reported scores move from 0.2600 (D2.8) to 0.2778 (B2), a +0.0178 difference. The repo's own analysis (README.md, H33-B2 section) also says the pruning can hurt when removed cells sit near withheld faults. Hidden credit and the hidden N are unavailable, so causality is not established.
  4. Can we beat 0.2778? Not demonstrated. The label-free candidate's HOLDOUT-DTI is 0.1294 (95% CI [0.1176, 0.1420], 60,988 withheld positives, evaluator gems-segment-pooled-v1). That is below the repo's recorded local point-best of 0.166060 (H24-S3DEP1, a non-unique file, DO_NOT_SUBMIT; docs/data/current_best_review.json). Proxy DTI values are not leaderboard scores, and the candidate's own catalogue-adjacent share is 20.5%, against B2's 5.8%.
