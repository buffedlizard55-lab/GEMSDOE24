# Four prospective fault-detection directions — not executed

**No lane is assigned and none of these is a predicted score or permission to run.** Ranking is a qualitative assessment of potential holdout information relative to implementation cost, not a DTI forecast. Results may be worse than existing methods. The pinned lattice imposes a separate, currently blocking geometric constraint; a model cannot be promoted merely by exceeding a holdout point estimate. [Feasibility analysis](protocol-feasibility-20261027.md) · [nine-site executive guide](executive-summary.html#current-focus-files).

| Rank | Layers and specific proposed signature | Why it could identify an unmapped fault / named non-fault mimic | Difference from repository work and relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Signed magnetic–gravity gradient reversal.** On fixed scales compute the signed cross-gradient of cached `rtp` and `iso_grav_anom`; test its reversal along an independently estimated `det_elev` line tangent. Use only valid neighborhoods. | An oblique intersection of buried structures may flip relative orientations across a splay obscured in the surface catalogue. **Mimics:** intrusive contacts, magnetic remanence and survey seams. | GVC1/EUL1 used unsigned co-alignment or magnitudes; TMCG used unsigned topography–magnetic cross-gradient. This tests a signed gravity–magnetic reversal. **Potential/cost:** first for information per cost, medium CPU/memory; actual DTI unknown. Identity of owner-mirrored input channels is not organizer-authenticated. |
| 2 | **Conditional ordinal potassium front.** Use the cached GeoDAWN `K`, `Th`, `U` uint8 grids; learn training-fold-only conditional K ranks given Th/U ranks, then measure the residual cross-front gradient where Th/U ranks vary little. | A localized compositional overprint near a buried discontinuity might mark a permeability corridor not traced in surface geology. **Mimics:** arkosic sediment sorting, moisture, vegetation and weathering of lithologic boundaries. | SAHG1 used fixed contrasts and CRP1 rank-space fronts; a fold-fitted conditional residual is different but related. **Potential/cost:** second, medium; actual DTI unknown. These are quantized proxies, not absolute concentrations or proof of alteration. |
| 3 | **Separated terrain-flexure dipole plus basement step.** Combine cached `det_elev`, `det_local_relief`, `depth_to_base_surf`, `geod_dilaterate`, and `geod_shearrate`; look for oppositely signed, separated cross-normal curvature lobes around a basement-depth gradient, with broad strain corroboration. | Flexure across a covered fault could persist where erosion has erased a sharp scarp. **Mimics:** terrace–valley pairs, alluvial fans, dunes and road cuts. | RSHG1 tested pointwise Hessian curvature and CON a conductivity saddle; this targets a separated signed pair. **Potential/cost:** third, medium; actual DTI unknown and 100 m resolution/coarse strain limit localization. |
| 4 | **Mineral-transition offset from a geophysical front.** Align USGS ASTER hydrothermal-alteration mineral-group maps with `rtp`, `iso_grav_anom`, or `cond_surf`; test whether an elongated alteration-type transition is consistently displaced to one side of an independently determined geophysical lineament. Validate alignment and valid support before use. | Mineralized fluid migration alongside a concealed structure can leave an offset alteration halo rather than a mappable scarp. **Mimics:** volcanic lithologic contacts, pre-existing mineralization, mine waste, atmospheric artifacts and vegetation. | Earlier SAHG/CRP/BLIND-UPWELL implementations used radiometry and thermal proxies; the unrun SENT2 concept proposes SWIR lineaments. This instead uses a published ASTER mineral-type **transition and its signed offset**; it is related work, not a claim of global novelty. **Potential/cost:** fourth because coverage and concordance are unproven, high acquisition/alignment/masking cost; actual DTI unknown. |

## Data availability and limitations

- The owner-mirrored `training_features.tif` and the 30-channel prepared stack were restored locally and hash-checked during this review. Their SHA-256 values are `4371c82e3b8339b807bdffcf4ef59a225520fe2988d521be208ae33743123bc5` and `a4ca25b2a18f6f6637f0247367cc6235a1366eb8475f77b18cd0285e62232dc8`, respectively. Hash consistency does **not** authenticate organizer provenance; both are Git-ignored bulk data and may require restoration later.
- The local `data/external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif` records `K`, `Th`, `U`, `TC`; the [official USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and), DOI `10.5066/P93LGLVQ`, lists magnetic/radiometric survey products and a CC0 designation. The published release is an official data source, but this session did not download a new official archive or independently verify which release tiles cover each competition cell. Flight spacing and clearance differ by area. Cache ranks are quantized; the disputed legacy band-6 label is not evidence of gravity or units.
- The [USGS ASTER western mineral/alteration data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/digital-maps-hydrothermal-alteration-type-key-mineral-groups-and-green-vegetation-western), DOI `10.5066/F7CR5RK7`, describes publicly available mineral-type maps and marks the release CC0. A separate [USGS Basin and Range ASTER metadata record](https://mrdata.usgs.gov/metadata/ofr-2013-1139.faq.html), DOI `10.3133/ofr20131139`, describes mineral-alteration polygons and regional bounds. These public descriptions were inspected, but no ASTER file was downloaded here: exact overlap with the competition footprint, per-file licensing, usability and automated access are **unverified**. Do not call that input data-ready.
- [USGS Battle Mountain blind-geothermal modeling](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271418) supports combining subsurface modalities when surface evidence is absent; it does not validate any proposal above. [USGS ASTER mineral mapping at Cuprite](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70025439) supports mapping alteration minerals, not the inference that a mineral pixel is a fault or a geothermal resource.

## Required protocol before any future test

Only a coordinator-provided **single method paragraph** can assign the lane. First resolve the retained-lattice policy without unilaterally weakening it; do not concentrate placements in lattice holes. Preregister the actual inputs and transformations, build all catalogue-derived features and eligibility masks solely from visible faults in each whole-segment buffered fold, and test each actual feature including missingness. A feature-only AUC over 0.90 in either polarity is suspected leakage until disproved. Compare the pre-placement surface against the complete current registry; stop on absolute rank correlation over 0.90. After placement stop if more than 70% of final candidate dots lie within 3 pixels of any one registry raster, including the lattice. Use pooled, pixel-exact visible-mask DTI with α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel; record the evaluator, withheld positives and 95% CI. Never treat passing a format check, an old unverified owner score, or an unreplayed source holdout as selector approval. Enforce the separate selector and verified account quota; use no competition slot during experiments. The budget remains at most three experiments or two hours.
