Why did GEMSDOE32's B2 report 0.2778?

01 · Ancestry (computed)

It is a deletion, not a detector

B2 (37,654 dots) is exactly the D2.8 mask (44,090, reported 0.2600) with every dot within 2 px of the public catalogue removed: 6,436 removed, 0 added (recomputed mask equality). The same rule also derives B2 from the H27-family mask (40,199 → −2,545). The reported gain is +0.0178 from deletion alone.

02 · Mechanism (metric algebra)

Halo dots pay full price, earn ~nothing

Catalogue pixels are masked exactly, but 1–2 px halo dots are not — each pays false-positive mass while the hidden-truth credit of the mapped trace's halo is near zero. In the lattice-calibrated sparse-truth model (hidden truth ≈12,632 px), the reported gain is consistent only with the deleted dots retaining ≈100% of nothing (implied credit retention 0.999–1.002; break-even 0.934).

Method, in one paragraph

CGMK1 tests whether an oblique buried splay rotates the magnetic-basement and gravity-basement gradient fields against each other (signed cross-gradient sin/cos + |∇sin| reversal, two scales) and whether potassium that cannot be explained by thorium/uranium lithology (fold-fit conditional residual front) marks concealed conduits — supervised on geophysics only, with the topography trunk and LiDAR excluded so the detector cannot win by re-reading the same scarps the catalogue was mapped from. It cannot (0.0887 vs 0.1661 trunk lanes), which quantifies how much of the internal holdout is same-modality rediscovery. Mimics that can produce the same signatures without a fault: intrusive contacts, remanent magnetization, survey seams, arkosic sediment sorting. Inputs: competition feature mirror (hash-verified, not organizer-authenticated) + USGS GeoDAWN radiometrics (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ). Preregistration, 5-hypothesis ranking, irregularities and the external-data viability check: 32_preregistered_CGMK1.
